## Research focus

The dissertation investigated a proposed low-power hybrid renewable-energy concept intended for remote and off-grid IoT applications. The concept combined solar PV and a Savonius-type vertical-axis wind turbine, with an electromagnetic arrangement, an active magnetic bearing (AMB) concept, a PMSG representation, battery storage and proposed supervisory control.

The abstract describes the research as being conducted primarily through mathematical calculations and theoretical explanations, with MATLAB/Simulink used to establish a simulation framework.

## Objectives stated in the dissertation

1. Conduct research on solar–wind hybrid technology.
2. Investigate the impact of magnetic levitation on wind-turbine performance.
3. Compare mechanical-bearing and active-magnetic-bearing arrangements.
4. Design a proposed hybrid model integrating the magnetic-bearing concept.
5. Develop a control-circuit concept for the hybrid model.
6. Perform simulations of the designed model.

## Main model elements

- 12 V, 56 W solar panel and MPPT control
- Savonius-type vertical-axis wind turbine
- Four permanent magnets and four electromagnets in the proposed rotor concept
- Active magnetic bearing concept
- PMSG representation
- Battery and buck-converter representation
- Proposed sensor-based supervisory logic, with Raspberry Pi as the intended controller platform

## Results and scope

The dissertation reports a mechanical-bearing versus AMB-related turbine comparison and a proposed hybrid-model power table in Chapter 5. The numerical results and interpretation caveats are summarised in the repository [README](../README.md) and [Code Notes](Code_Notes.md).

The dissertation's Chapter 7 identifies further work in magnetic-field interaction analysis, integrated control-system simulation including the Raspberry Pi module, and hardware setup of the proposed model and control system. These items should therefore be treated as future work, not completed outcomes.
